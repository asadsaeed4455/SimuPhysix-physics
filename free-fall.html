Projectile Motion

Difficulty: Beginner–IntermediateKinematics

A projectile moves under gravity alone. Its horizontal motion is uniform and its vertical motion is uniformly accelerated, and combining them gives a parabola. Use the lab below to launch objects and watch every quantity update.

Complete Physics Lecture

1. Introduction

Throw a ball, kick a football, or fire water from a hose: once the object leaves your hand, the only force acting on it (ignoring air) is its weight. The path it follows is called a trajectory, and the object is a projectile. Projectile motion is the standard example of two-dimensional kinematics with constant acceleration.

2. Definition

Projectile motion is the motion of an object that is launched with an initial velocity and then moves only under the influence of gravity. Its acceleration is constant and equal to g, directed vertically downward.

3. Physical principles

  • Independence of motions: the horizontal and vertical motions do not affect each other. They share only the time t.
  • Horizontal: no force, so ax = 0 and vx is constant.
  • Vertical: weight only, so ay = −g (upward is positive).
  • Energy: with no drag, mechanical energy is conserved: ½v² + g·y is constant for every kilogram.

4. Step-by-step method

  1. Choose axes: x horizontal, y upward, origin at the launch point on the ground (or h₀ above it).
  2. Split v₀ into components.
  3. Write x(t) and y(t).
  4. Find the time using y = 0 (landing) or vy = 0 (apex).
  5. Substitute that time to get the distance or the other component.

5. Important equations

vₓ₀ = v₀ cos θ    vy0 = v₀ sin θ
x(t) = v₀ cos θ · t
y(t) = h₀ + v₀ sin θ · t − ½ g t²
vy(t) = v₀ sin θ − g t
tapex = v₀ sin θ / g    H = h₀ + (v₀ sin θ)² / (2g)
T = [ v₀ sin θ + √( (v₀ sin θ)² + 2 g h₀ ) ] / g
R = v₀ cos θ · T    (h₀ = 0: R = v₀² sin 2θ / g)
y = h₀ + x tan θ − g x² / (2 v₀² cos² θ)

7. Derivations

Components. From the right triangle of the launch velocity, vₓ₀ = v₀ cos θ and vy0 = v₀ sin θ.

Time of flight. The ball lands when y = 0: h₀ + vy0t − ½gt² = 0. This is a quadratic ½gt² − vy0t − h₀ = 0, and the physical (positive) root is t = [vy0 + √(vy0² + 2gh₀)] / g. For h₀ = 0 this becomes T = 2v₀ sin θ / g.

Maximum height. At the top vy = 0, so t = vy0/g. Substituting into y(t) gives H = h₀ + vy0²/(2g).

Range on level ground. R = (v₀ cos θ)(2v₀ sin θ / g) = v₀² (2 sin θ cos θ) / g = v₀² sin 2θ / g. Since sin 2θ is largest at 2θ = 90°, the maximum range occurs at θ = 45°. Angles θ and 90° − θ give the same range.

Parabola. Eliminating t = x/(v₀ cos θ) from y(t) gives y = h₀ + x tan θ − gx²/(2v₀² cos² θ), which is a parabola.

Impact speed. By energy conservation, v² = v₀² + 2gh₀, independent of the launch angle.

8. Physical interpretation

Gravity changes only vy, by 9.8 m/s every second on Earth. The ball slows while rising, stops vertically at the apex, then speeds up while falling. The speed at the apex is not zero, because vₓ is still there. On the Moon g is six times smaller, so the same throw goes about six times farther.

9. Everyday applications

Sports (basketball, football, javelin, long jump), water fountains and fire hoses, sprinklers, artillery and rocketry design, and video-game physics.

10. Common misconceptions

  • "A force keeps pushing the ball forward." No. After launch only gravity acts, and the horizontal velocity stays constant because there is no horizontal force.
  • "At the top the acceleration is zero." No. Velocity in y is zero but ay = −g throughout.
  • "Heavier objects fall faster or fly differently." Without air resistance, mass does not appear in any equation.
  • "Higher angle always means longer range." Range is greatest at 45° (for h₀ = 0) and decreases beyond it.

11. What to observe in the simulation

  • Set θ = 30° and then 60° and use Keep trace. The ranges are equal.
  • Change g to Moon: R grows by 9.8/1.62 ≈ 6.
  • Pause at the apex: vy = 0 but v = vₓ, and ay still shows −g.
  • In the Energy tab, KE and PE trade places but the total stays constant.
  • Raise h₀: the impact speed increases, and the range and impact angle change too.

Worked Numerical Examples

Example 1: Ball kicked from the ground

  1. Given: v₀ = 20 m/s, θ = 30°, h₀ = 0, g = 9.8 m/s².
  2. Required: time of flight T, maximum height H, range R.
  3. Equations: T = 2v₀ sin θ / g, H = (v₀ sin θ)² / (2g), R = vₓ₀ T.
  4. Components: vy0 = 20 sin 30° = 10 m/s; vₓ₀ = 20 cos 30° = 17.32 m/s.
  5. Calculation: T = 2(10)/9.8 = 2.04 s. H = 10² / (2 × 9.8) = 100/19.6 = 5.10 m. R = 17.32 × 2.04 = 35.3 m.
Answer: T = 2.04 s, H = 5.10 m, R = 35.3 m.

Example 2: Ball thrown horizontally from a cliff

  1. Given: h₀ = 45 m, v₀ = 15 m/s horizontal (θ = 0°), g = 9.8 m/s².
  2. Required: time to land, horizontal distance, impact speed and angle.
  3. Equations: h₀ = ½gT², R = v₀T, vy = −gT, v = √(vₓ² + vy²).
  4. Time: T = √(2h₀/g) = √(90/9.8) = √9.184 = 3.03 s.
  5. Distance: R = 15 × 3.03 = 45.5 m.
  6. Impact: |vy| = 9.8 × 3.03 = 29.7 m/s, v = √(15² + 29.7²) = √1107 = 33.3 m/s, angle = tan⁻¹(29.7/15) = 63.2° below horizontal.
Answer: T = 3.03 s, R = 45.5 m, v = 33.3 m/s at 63.2° below horizontal.

Learning Assessment

Conceptual questions

  1. Why does the horizontal velocity of an ideal projectile stay constant?
  2. What are the velocity and acceleration of a projectile at the top of its path?
  3. Two balls are launched at 30° and 60° with equal speed from level ground. Compare their ranges and times of flight.
  4. Does a heavier ball travel farther than a lighter one in a vacuum? Explain.
  5. A ball is dropped and another is thrown horizontally at the same instant from the same height. Which lands first?

Numerical questions (take g = 9.8 m/s²)

  1. Find the range of a projectile launched at 30 m/s and 45° from level ground.
  2. Find the maximum height for v₀ = 40 m/s at 60°.
  3. Find the time of flight for v₀ = 25 m/s at 37° (sin 37° ≈ 0.6) from level ground.
  4. A stone is thrown horizontally at 10 m/s from a 20 m high roof. How far from the base does it land?
  5. Find the range of a 20 m/s, 45° launch on the Moon (g = 1.62 m/s²).
Show answers and explanations

Conceptual

  1. No horizontal force acts (air resistance ignored), so ax = 0 and vx does not change.
  2. Velocity is purely horizontal (v = vₓ, with vy = 0). Acceleration is still g = 9.8 m/s² downward.
  3. Ranges are equal because sin 60° = sin 120°, i.e. the angles are complementary. The 60° ball stays up longer (T ∝ sin θ), so it has the longer time of flight and reaches a greater height.
  4. No. Mass cancels out of every projectile equation, so the paths are identical without air resistance.
  5. They land at the same time. Both have vy0 = 0 and the same vertical motion; the horizontal velocity does not affect it.

Numerical

  1. R = v₀² sin 2θ / g = 900 × 1 / 9.8 = 91.8 m.
  2. vy0 = 40 sin 60° = 34.64 m/s; H = 34.64² / 19.6 = 1200 / 19.6 = 61.2 m.
  3. vy0 = 25 × 0.6 = 15 m/s; T = 2(15)/9.8 = 3.06 s.
  4. t = √(2 × 20 / 9.8) = 2.02 s; x = 10 × 2.02 = 20.2 m.
  5. R = 400 × 1 / 1.62 = 247 m (about 6 times the 40.8 m on Earth).
